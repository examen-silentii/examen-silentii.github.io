Reviewing for ExSil means checking an examination by repeating it. This page explains what reviewers do and what the journal expects of them.

## What reviewers do

1. **Declare any conflict of interest** before starting. If you have worked on, funded, competed with or been in dispute with the software, data or people involved, tell the handling editor.
2. **Rerun the test** from the authors' archived materials, following their instructions as written. Record your environment and whether your results match theirs.
3. **Check the requirements** using the [reviewer checklist](https://github.com/examen-silentii/submissions/blob/main/REVIEW_CHECKLIST.md), posted as a comment on the submission issue.
4. **Judge the claims.** Do the conclusions follow from the results? Is the reference truly independent? Are the size and limits of the problem stated accurately?
5. **Recommend a decision**: accept, minor revisions, major revisions or reject, with reasons.

## Standards of conduct

- Review the work, not the people. Comments about the authors or the maintainers' competence have no place in a review.
- Be specific. Point to the exact section, result or step that needs attention, and say what would resolve it.
- Remember that the maintainers of the examined software may read every word. Errors in widely used tools are common and usually honest; the journal's purpose is to document them accurately, not to embarrass anyone.
- Reviews are public and signed with your GitHub account.

## Timing

Reviewers are asked to complete their review within four weeks of accepting the invitation. If you cannot, tell the handling editor as early as possible.

## Volunteering

To volunteer as a reviewer, open an issue with the *Volunteer to review* form in the [submissions repository](https://github.com/examen-silentii/submissions/issues/new/choose).
